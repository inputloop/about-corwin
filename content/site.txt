== about ==
I am a systems engineer and software developer. I have written software for a living for about twenty-five years, most of it in Emacs. I volunteer for GNU and the Free Software Foundation, and I am one of the Savannah Hackers, the volunteers who keep GNU's project hosting running. I am also a volunteer site reliability engineer with the FSF's sysops team, and I help maintain Worg, the Org mode community's documentation site. These days most of what I do is help other people get the same tools working on machines they keep.

I come from a family of writers, organizers and fans. My dad is the writer [Steven Brust](https://dreamcafe.com/). My grandparents, [Bill Brust](https://www.wsws.org/en/special/library/fi-19-1/15.html) and [Jean Brust](https://mehring.com/product/defending-principles-the-political-legacy-of-bill-brust/), organized for socialism in Minnesota for most of their lives. I am married to [Dee Brust](https://www.twinpropjane.com/), a folk musician and a retired ELL/ESL teacher of children from kindergarten through high school. I have been a science fiction fan all my life, and I have run a [Minicon](https://mnstf.org/minicon/), the Minneapolis science fiction convention. Some of that rubbed off: I like tools that people can share, and I like to show my work.

My work covers a few things that look different from the outside. I build the Windows release of GNU Emacs. I help organize EmacsConf. I look after one small corner of Org mode, its Perl support. I co-founded a game engine for tabletop dungeon crawls that runs inside Emacs. I also publish [card-games](https://melpa.org/#/card-games), an Emacs package you can install from MELPA. The common thread is a habit of asking what a newcomer needs, writing that down, and then checking the written thing against what really happens.

As of 2026-11-01, most of my time goes to inputloop, the small free software shop I keep with Bryant Frankford. The shop also carries Brigidt's NextTime, a time-banking app for Nextcloud, and Bryant's Quantum 3D5. It has its own site now, corwin.cloud, whose [source is public](https://github.com/inputloop/corwin.cloud): a diagram of how our machines connect, a directory of what we run, and a few curated packages.

If there is one thread through all of this, it is joining things up: one tool to another, one project to the people who could use it. When I start something I ask who it lets in, and I try to say plainly what is finished and what is not.
