Hi. I'm a systems engineer, and I write software. I've done it for a living for about twenty-five years. Most of it was in Emacs. Emacs is a text editor. It's also sort of my home. I help GNU and the Free Software Foundation. I'm one of the Savannah Hackers. We keep GNU's project hosting up. I'm a site reliability engineer for the FSF, too. That's a big name for "I keep the computers happy." I also help with Worg. It's the help site for Org mode. Mostly, I help you get these tools going on your own computer.

I come from a family of educators and writers, fans and scientists who organize. My dad is the writer [Steven](https://dreamcafe.com/) [Brust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Brust). Their parents, [Jean](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/jean-t-brust/) and [Bill](https://www.wsws.org/en/special/library/fi-19-1/15.html) Brust, worked for socialism in Minnesota. They did it for most of their lives. I'm married to [Dee Brust](https://www.twinpropjane.com/). We married in 2006. Dee is a folk musician. She's a retired ELL/ESL teacher, too. She taught kids from kindergarten all the way up to high school. We have five kids! One is out of college. One is just starting college. The rest are in primary school. I've been a science fiction fan all my life. Minicon is the science fiction con in Minneapolis. I co-chaired [Minicon 37](https://mnstf.org/minicon/) in 2002. Some of that rubbed off on me. I like tools that folks can share. And I like to show my work.

My work looks like lots of things. I build GNU Emacs for Windows. I help run EmacsConf. I look after a tiny bit of Org mode: the bit for Perl. I helped start a game. You play it in Emacs, and you crawl through a dungeon. I made [card-games](https://melpa.org/#/card-games), too. You can get it from MELPA. What ties it all up? I ask what a new person needs. I write it down. Then I check it. Does it really work? I like to know.

On 2026-11-01, most of my time goes to inputloop. It's a small free software shop. I keep it with Bryant Frankford. We carry Brigidt's NextTime, too. It's an app for Nextcloud, and you can use it to trade time. And we carry Bryant's Quantum 3D5. The shop has its own site now: corwin.cloud. Its [code is public](https://github.com/inputloop/corwin.cloud). It shows how our computers link up. It lists what we run. It has a few tools we picked.

Here's the big thread. I like to join things up. One tool to the next. One project to the folks who need it. When I start a thing, I ask: who does it let in? I try to say what's done and what's not.
