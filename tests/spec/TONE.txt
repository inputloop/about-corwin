TONE: the rules the tone d-pad's checks share. build_tone.py (Python) and tests/js/tone.test.mjs
(JavaScript) are written from these words, separately; each must reach the same answers.

1. THE CELLS
   Warmth has three steps: warm, plain, formal. Reading has four: grade 4, grade 8, grade 12,
   college (written 16). A text's cell is a pair, such as "warm 4". The base cell is "plain 8":
   the words already on the page. (Measured 2026-11-01: the About read grade 6.4 by
   Flesch-Kincaid and 6.7 by Coleman-Liau, so today's text is the grade-8 step, not grade 12.)

2. BEFORE MEASURING
   Turn [words](https://...) into "words". Drop a paragraph that is only [[keep]] (the kept
   paragraph is the same in every cell and is not measured). Lower-case the text.
   A WORD is a run of letters a-z, with an apostrophe allowed between letters.
   A SENTENCE ends at each run of . ! or ? that follows a word, and at each line end that follows
   a word since the last end. A paragraph is one line.
   SYLLABLES (Flesch-Kincaid only): drop apostrophes, count runs of a e i o u y; if the word ends in
   e but not le and the count is above 1, take one away; never below 1. (Same as the voice palace's
   readability/SPEC.md, from which fk.py was copied.)

3. THE TWO READING METERS (they share no method)
   Python, Flesch-Kincaid grade: 0.39 x words/sentences + 11.8 x syllables/words - 15.59.
   JavaScript, Coleman-Liau index: 0.0588 x L - 0.296 x S - 15.8, where L is letters per 100 words
   and S is sentences per 100 words (letters are a-z inside words; apostrophes do not count).
   The rule: within each text, at each warmth, the four steps rise on both meters
   (grade 4 < grade 8 < grade 12 < college). For the About only, each step also sits in its band
   on both meters: grade 4 below 5.5; grade 8 from 5 to 9.5; grade 12 from 9 to 13.5; college
   12.5 or more. Short lead-ins (one or two sentences) are held to order only: a meter cannot
   place one sentence in a grade.

4. THE WARMTH COUNT (direction only)
   Points, counted over the words:
     +3 each contraction: a word ending in n't, 're, 've, 'll, 'm or 'd, or one of it's, that's,
        there's, here's, what's, let's, who's;
     +2 each you, your, yours, you're, you've, you'll;
     +1 each i, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours;
     +2 each of these: love, like, likes, fun, glad, happy, enjoy, hope, joy, friend, friends,
        friendly, lucky, really, honestly, okay, hey, yes;
     +3 each question mark, +2 each exclamation mark;
     -2 each word of 7 letters or more ending in tion, tions, ment, ments, ness, ity, ities, ance,
        ence (nominalisations: things said as nouns).
   Warmth = points x 100 / words, rounded to one decimal. The rule: at every reading step,
   warm > plain > formal, each by at least 1.0. Whether a cell sounds like Corwin is his read, not
   this count's.

5. SAME FACTS
   A text declares its facts (fact: UNIT | words). Every cell holds every fact, exactly as written.
   Nothing is added: every link address, every run of digits, and every capitalised word that does
   not start a sentence must already appear in the base text or in an extra (extra: UNIT | words),
   or be "I". (A word starts a sentence when it is the first word of a paragraph, or follows . ! ?
   : or an opening quote mark.)

6. THE VOICE RULE
   Cells speak as "I" for Corwin and "we" for the shop. Only formal cells may name "Corwin Brust"
   in the third person (Corwin, 2026-11-01: "no holds barred"); he, him and his stay out of every
   cell except inside quotation marks.

7. A MISSING CELL
   Asked for warmth w and step g: try (w, g); then (w one step toward plain, g). If neither is
   written, move g one step toward 8 and try again the same way. Reaching (plain, 8), use the base.
   No cell can show empty. The build writes this table into the page; tone.model.js works it out
   again in the browser, and a check holds that the two agree for every text and every cell.

8. THE BROWSER'S LANGUAGE (Corwin, 2026-11-02: "Browser Language: SOLD. Default ON")
   Only a reader with no saved choice is affected. Take the browser's list of languages (in the
   browser, navigator.languages; if that is empty, navigator.language) and look at the FIRST one
   only. Its language is the part before the first - or _, in lower case. If there is no first
   language, or its language is not two or three letters a-z, or it is "und" (undetermined),
   start as written. If it is "en", in any variety (en-US, en-GB, en-IN ...), start as written.
   Otherwise start one reading step simpler at plain warmth: plain 4. There is no step below 4,
   so "two steps" cannot happen. The page then says so in one quiet line, naming the language in
   English. A saved choice always wins; moving a pad or a row makes the choice the reader's; the
   Customize row "Follow my browser's language" (on unless turned off, kept in this browser) stops
   it. Both readers check these cases, written "languages -> start":
   case: de-DE -> grade 4
   case: de -> grade 4
   case: fr_CA -> grade 4
   case: zh-Hant-TW -> grade 4
   case: es-419, en -> grade 4
   case: de, en -> grade 4
   case: en-GB -> as written
   case: en-IN -> as written
   case: EN-us -> as written
   case: en, de -> as written
   case: en -> as written
   case: und -> as written
   case: (none) -> as written
